About This Project

About NeuroVoice AI

A voice-based early detection system for Parkinson's Disease built on peer-reviewed vocal biomarker research.

The Science Behind It

Parkinson's Disease (PD) affects over 10 million people worldwide. Research has established that vocal biomarkers — measurable characteristics of a person's voice — change significantly in individuals with PD due to impaired motor control of the vocal cords (dysarthria).

This system uses the same acoustic features studied in the landmark Oxford Parkinson's Disease Detection Dataset (Little et al., 2007), which demonstrated that voice measurements can be used to discriminate healthy individuals from those with Parkinson's with high accuracy.

Acoustic Features Analyzed

Pitch Features (F0)

Mean, maximum, and minimum fundamental frequency of the voice. PD patients show greater pitch variability and lower mean pitch.

Jitter

Cycle-to-cycle variation in pitch period. Higher jitter values indicate instability in vocal cord vibration, a key PD marker.

Shimmer

Cycle-to-cycle variation in amplitude. Elevated shimmer reflects reduced vocal cord tension control in PD.

HNR / NHR

Harmonics-to-Noise Ratio measures the ratio of periodic (harmonic) to noise components. PD voices typically have lower HNR.

RPDE / DFA / PPE

Nonlinear dynamical complexity measures capturing subtle irregularities in vocal fold vibration patterns not captured by linear measures.

MFCCs (13)

Mel-Frequency Cepstral Coefficients represent the spectral envelope of the voice and are widely used in speech disorder analysis.

⚠️ Important Medical Disclaimer

NeuroVoice AI is a research and educational tool designed to demonstrate the potential of AI in healthcare screening. It is NOT an FDA-approved medical device and should NOT be used for clinical diagnosis or treatment decisions.

If you or someone you know is experiencing symptoms of Parkinson's Disease (tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, postural instability), please seek evaluation from a qualified neurologist immediately.
